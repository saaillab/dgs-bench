Abstract

Agricultural AI models trained on balanced laboratory datasets routinely report moderate linear-probe accuracy (~55%), yet the same models appear to improve when evaluated on naturally imbalanced African field data. We show that this apparent improvement is an Evaluation Mirage: class imbalance inflates aggregate accuracy even as minority-class performance degrades. We introduce DGS-Bench, a reproducible evaluation framework using the public Kaggle cassava disease dataset, and demonstrate the mirage across 40 independent experiments (10 random seeds x 4 pretrained architectures): raw-accuracy DGS is negative in all 40 cases (mean -6.00 +/- 2.38 pp; t = -15.8, p < 1e-6), whereas macro-F1 DGS is positive in all 40 (mean +8.16 +/- 2.34 pp; t = 21.7, p < 1e-6). Three additional lines of evidence strengthen the finding: (1) image-space background removal via GrabCut segmentation collapses field accuracy by 20-35 pp, confirming feature-space perturbation results with direct pixel-level evidence; (2) fine-tuning ResNet-50 (full and last-block) amplifies the mirage rather than eliminating it; and (3) cross-architecture threshold analysis reveals that the mirage onset varies from 20% to 50% dominant-class prevalence depending on architecture. Macro-AUPRC DGS is positive for all architectures (mean +8.3 pp), confirming the F1 finding; macro-AUROC DGS reverses sign (negative), highlighting the sharp distinction between ranking and threshold-dependent metrics under imbalance. Cross-dataset validation on PlantVillage Tomato (10 classes) and Rice Leaf Disease (4 classes) confirms that the mirage requires both severe class imbalance and moderate model uncertainty; it does not appear when frozen features achieve >90% lab accuracy.

Results

Across all 40 seed-architecture runs on Ugandan field cassava data, raw accuracy appears higher in the field (-6.00 +/- 2.38 pp DGS_Acc, t = -15.8, p < 1e-6), whereas balanced Macro-F1 exposes consistent deployment loss across every model (+8.16 +/- 2.34 pp DGS_F1, t = 21.7, p < 1e-6), with Healthy leaf recall dropping from 52.5% to 47.7% and Cassava Bacterial Blight (5.1% field share) reaching only 28.4% AP against 93.8% AP on CMD. Background scrambling reduces accuracy by 13.6 to 21.3 percentage points across CNNs and Vision Transformers compared with less than 1.7 percentage points under brightness shifts, and v1.1 calibration plus multi-backbone fine-tuning extensions confirm 12/12 mirage persistence and up to -16.87 pp raw accuracy illusion under EM prior adaptation.

Theoretical Mirage Inequality & Mandatory Algorithm 1 Reporting Protocol

Closed-form onset condition under prior shift paired with the five mandatory audit rules (p < 1e-6 empirical mirage threshold).

p* = (Acc_L - r_bar_{-1}) / (r_1 - r_bar_{-1})
  1. Train candidate model f_theta strictly on balanced D_L^train without leaking natural D_F samples.
  2. Evaluate aggregate accuracy, Macro-F1, Balanced Accuracy, Macro-AUROC, and Macro-AUPRC on both balanced D_L^test and uncurated D_F.
  3. Compute balanced gaps DGS_F1 = F1_L - F1_F and DGS_AUPRC = AUPRC_L - AUPRC_F alongside raw DGS_Acc.
  4. Emit WARNING: report DGS_F1 as primary metric whenever majority-class prevalence exceeds 0.30 of |D_F|.
  5. Publish the complete per-class confusion matrix and empirical prevalence vector alongside aggregate headline numbers.

Under balanced lab evaluation, prediction errors distribute relatively evenly across classes; under natural 65.8% CMD prevalence, linear heads bias predictions toward CMD, artificially lifting overall field hit rates from 54.75% to 62.80% despite falling minority recall.

v1.1 Extension A: 10-Seed Temperature Scaling & Saerens EM Prior Adjustment

To test whether post hoc probability calibration or expectation-maximization prior shift correction resolves the evaluation mirage, we evaluated all four architectures across 10 seeds (40 runs) using out-of-fold Temperature Scaling fit strictly on balanced training logits and Saerens EM evaluated against the natural field split (true held-out CMD prior 65.77%).

Temperature scaling lowers field ECE by 81.7% (0.2721 to 0.0497) with zero class-ranking change (DGS_Acc remains -5.86 pp). Pairing calibrated probabilities with Saerens EM recovers the true field prevalence (68.02% +/- 3.58% vs. 65.77%) and cuts ECE to 0.0371 (Brier 0.3853), yet worsens the raw accuracy mirage to -16.87 pp and expands Macro-F1 loss to +12.01 pp because EM concentrates probability mass on CMD.

v1.1 Extension B: Grad-CAM & ViT Spatial Attribution Before/After GrabCut

Across n = 200 stratified field images filtered for non-degenerate GrabCut foliage masks (40 per class; mean leaf mask area 30.59%, outer 40% border area 63.14%), only 30.39% to 36.84% of positive Grad-CAM and ViT gradient attribution falls inside the leaf mask on original images, whereas 56.29% to 63.78% sits on the outer image border.

When central leaf pixels are replaced with neutral gray (Border-only), ResNet-50 drops to 24.0% accuracy (2.0% CMD prediction rate), whereas EfficientNet-B3 maintains 40.0% accuracy and its full 16.5% CMD prediction rate by placing 66.00% of attribution energy onto outer background soil and foliage clutter.
