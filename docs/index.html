DGS-Bench: Measuring the Deployment Gap in African Agricultural AI Requires Balanced Metrics: A Cassava Case Study

Innocent Nyalala 1,2 , Surabhi Gudla 1 , Patrick Vincent Ndowo 1
1School of Engineering and Science, Indian Institute of Technology Madras Zanzibar, Tanzania
2Wadhwani School of Data Science and AI, Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai, India
Deep Learning Indaba 2026, Pan-Atlantic University, Lagos, Nigeria
Best Poster Award, Deep Learning Indaba 2026 (Lagos, Nigeria)

Agricultural vision models evaluated on balanced laboratory test sets often appear to improve when tested on naturally imbalanced African field data, misleading practitioners about deployment readiness. Across 40 controlled experiments spanning four architectures (ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B3, MobileNetV3-S, ViT-Small/16) and 10 random seeds on Ugandan field cassava imagery (65.8% CMD dominance), DGS-Bench exposes this artifact as an Evaluation Mirage: raw-accuracy Deployment Gap Score turns negative (-6.00 ± 2.38 pp) while Macro-F1 exposes a true +8.16 ± 2.34 pp degradation. Pixel-space GrabCut background removal and cross-dataset validation on Tomato and Rice show how majority-class skew and background clutter conceal silent minority-class failures.

Abstract

Agricultural AI models trained on balanced laboratory datasets routinely report moderate linear-probe accuracy (~55%), yet the same models appear to improve when evaluated on naturally imbalanced African field data. We show that this apparent improvement is an Evaluation Mirage: class imbalance inflates aggregate accuracy while minority-class performance degrades. We introduce DGS-Bench, a reproducible evaluation framework using the public Kaggle cassava disease dataset, and demonstrate the mirage across 40 independent experiments (10 random seeds x 4 pretrained architectures): raw-accuracy DGS is negative in all 40 cases (mean -6.00 +/- 2.38 pp; t = -15.8, p < 1e-6), while macro-F1 DGS is positive in all 40 (mean +8.16 +/- 2.34 pp; t = 21.7, p < 1e-6). Three additional lines of evidence strengthen the finding: (1) image-space background removal via GrabCut segmentation collapses field accuracy by 20-35 pp, confirming feature-space perturbation results with direct pixel-level evidence; (2) fine-tuning ResNet-50 (full and last-block) amplifies the mirage rather than eliminating it; and (3) cross-architecture threshold analysis reveals that the mirage onset varies from 20% to 50% dominant-class prevalence depending on architecture. Macro-AUPRC DGS is positive for all architectures (mean +8.3 pp), confirming the F1 finding; macro-AUROC DGS diverges (negative), highlighting the critical distinction between ranking and threshold-dependent metrics under imbalance. Cross-dataset validation on PlantVillage Tomato (10 classes) and Rice Leaf Disease (4 classes) confirms that the mirage requires both severe class imbalance and moderate model uncertainty; it does not appear when frozen features achieve >90% lab accuracy. Results

Across all 40 seed-architecture runs on Ugandan field cassava data, raw accuracy appears higher in the field (-6.00 ± 2.38 pp DGS_Acc, t = -15.8, p < 1e-6), whereas balanced Macro-F1 exposes consistent deployment loss across every model (+8.16 ± 2.34 pp DGS_F1, t = 21.7, p < 1e-6), with Healthy leaf recall dropping from 52.5% to 47.7%. Background scrambling reduces accuracy by 13.6 to 21.3 percentage points across CNNs and Vision Transformers compared with less than 1.7 percentage points under brightness changes, and cross-crop replication confirms the mirage appears whenever severe imbalance coincides with moderate lab accuracy.
